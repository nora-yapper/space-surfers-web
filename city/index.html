How the city is built

Nothing in the city is placed by hand. Before a ride, the game plans the whole street from the song, then builds it from a small kit of pieces made from our models. Every piece below is shown three ways: the sketch it started as, the piece built on its own by the game's code, and the same piece in the game.

Contents: The street · Wall chunks · Across the street · Street crossings · Ground · How the song places them · The three cities

The street

Looking down the track. The flying space, where the trail and the board can go, is 16 m wide and 22 m tall, and it slowly rises 120 m over a song. Building faces stand just outside it, so nothing in the city can block the trail. Behind every wall stand two more rows of buildings and a far skyline, and the buildings reach 80 m below the street into the haze, so you never see where they end.

Wall chunks

Six kinds of wall, each 30 to 54 m of street. The game lays out each side of the street on its own, chunk after chunk, and picks every chunk from how loud the song is at that spot. Loudness is averaged over 4 seconds, so the walls change with the song's sections, not with single beats.

  • Quiet, middle, loud: under 35% of the song's loudness, 35 to 70%, and above 70%.
  • Never twice in a row: a wall is never followed by the same kind on its side of the street.
  • Roof height: towers, pipe walls, hanging blocks and sign alleys rise 10 m above the flying space when it's quiet, up to 45 m when it's loud. Tenements and tank gaps stay low, between 6 m under and 4 m over its top, so light comes in.
  • Anchors: an anchor is a fixed point on a building where something can be attached, like a hook in a wall. They are the blue dots in the sketches. Cables may only start and end at an anchor, so no cable ever ends in mid-air. Each tall wall gets four anchors and each low one gets two, all on the face above the flying space, plus one at the top of the pole on the roof of towers, tenements and tank gaps.
  • Cables: each joins a left anchor to a right anchor at most 8 m further along the street, face to face or pole to pole, and never sags into the flying space. About a third of anchors get none, so the sky never fills up.
  • Both sides: the same chunks, mirrored, with the building sometimes turned around. Behind each one the game adds two back rows and a far skyline, and about two thirds of the buildings get a colour wash so copies don't look the same.

Across the street

Big pieces that join the two buildings, always outside the flying space, so they mark the song without ever being in your way. They follow the song's structure:

  • When: one at every section change the song analysis finds (for example verse to chorus). Where a stretch of the song has no section change for 14 seconds, the game adds one anyway, so you never go long without one.
  • Which: they take turns in a fixed order: block above, skybridge, gantry, pipe bridge, block above, cable web, tram line, block below, then round again. That's why the block above shows up twice as often.
  • How high: 4 to 9 m above the top of the flying space. The block below sits under the flying space, in the haze.
  • Held up: every one reaches deep into both buildings or stands on columns that go down 70 m, so nothing hangs in the air.

Street crossings

Smaller pieces that cross inside the flying space, the part of the street you ride through. They follow the trail, which itself comes from the song's loudness and brightness:

  • When: the game tries one every 9 seconds or so (6 to 13 s, so they don't feel like a metronome).
  • Where: it looks at where the trail goes for 1.5 seconds before and after. A crossing goes in only if it fits at least 7 m below or above the trail over that whole stretch, measured from its full height. If there's no room, it tries again a second later.
  • Which: they take turns: crates, catwalk, containers, crates. Crates are the smallest, so they come up most.
  • Never hit: the 7 m clearance means you ride past them close up but never into them. They are scenery that makes the street feel lived in, not obstacles.

Ground

What is under you, laid out in 24 m pieces from the start of the song to the end:

  • Usually: half of all pieces are grate deck, and the fine mesh, pipe deck and rubble street share the other half equally.
  • Quiet parts: where the song is quiet (under 35% loudness), about a third of the pieces become rubble street or pipe deck instead, so calm stretches look more worn and solid.
  • Below the deck: under the see-through grate and mesh, the city keeps going with faint lit windows far down.

How the song places them

  • Loudness picks the walls and how tall they are, and how much the ground is grate or rubble.
  • Section changes place the big pieces across the street, above or below you.
  • The trail decides where the street crossings fit around you.
  • The whole song lifts the flying space 120 m, faster when it is loud, so you rise away from the ground.
  • Not random each time: the random choices come from a seed made from the song's name, so every ride of a song builds exactly the same city, and each difficulty's song builds a different one.

The three cities

Each difficulty has its own song, so its own city. The numbers come straight from the game's city plan.
